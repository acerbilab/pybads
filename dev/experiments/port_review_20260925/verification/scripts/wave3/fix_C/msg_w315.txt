fix: the ES search ranks tied candidates and training points in their order, with MATLAB's stable sort (W3-15)

The ES search ranks its pool of candidates by acquisition value, and
ES-wcm the GP's training points by value, with np.argsort, whose
default quicksort does not keep the order of equal values; MATLAB's
sort is stable. Ties are common in the pool: far candidates whose
predictions equal the GP's prior exactly, and they rank near the top.
Which of the tied candidates are kept, and the order of the parents,
which decides their offspring, then depended on NumPy's sort (and
possibly on the CPU, if its SIMD sort orders ties differently).

Both sorts are now stable (kind="stable"), at the pool of the ES
(__call__) and at ES-wcm's best training points (_initialize_), so that
equal values keep their order in the pool (earlier generations first,
contraints_check's order within each) and in the training set, as in
MATLAB's searchES (sort(z) and sort(Y,'ascend')). With the pool
untrimmed (W3-8), the order among ties matches MATLAB's up to the
second generation; from the third, a candidate that MATLAB trimmed at a
tie could still rank before the new ones of equal value.

Test: test_search.py::
test_es_wcm_ranks_tied_training_points_in_their_order (training values
in {0, 1, 2}: the best points passed to ucov are those of Python's
stable sort, in its order) and
test_es_search_ranks_tied_candidates_in_their_order (acquisition values
in {-1, 0}: the search returns the first of the best in the pool). Both
fail at the parent and pass here.

Fingerprint (dev/scripts/fingerprint.py, Linux, gpyreg v1.3.3):
3a6c31fe4f430b0b, the parent's (W3-5): the six runs meet ties that the
two sorts order differently (at 87 of 294 pools, 38 of them in the
first generation), but their whole evaluation histories are unchanged.
The change does move runs: three seeded runs of a 3-D Rosenbrock
function (bounds [-5, 5], 120 evaluations) all have other histories,
two of them another final value.
