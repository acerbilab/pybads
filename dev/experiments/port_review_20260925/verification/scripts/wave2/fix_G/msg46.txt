fix: no overflow warning from the masked exponential of a linear variable beside a log-scaled one (W2-46)

For a mix of log-scaled and linear variables, the inverse of
VariableTransformer's transform takes the exponential of every variable,
clamps it at the largest float and masks out the linear ones, as MATLAB
BADS's transvars.m does (lines 157-158). The exponential of a linear
variable at a value above about 709 overflows: NumPy warns, where MATLAB
gives Inf silently, and the masked value is discarded. The self-test of
the transform at construction meets it for a linear variable with an
infinite bound (tested at +-1/sqrt(eps)) or a bound above about 709 in
magnitude, so that BADS(...) printed "RuntimeWarning: overflow
encountered in exp" once; so does the inverse of each point of a run
whose linear variable is above 709. Since W2-1 and W2-2 accept mixed and
half-bounded problems, such problems meet the warning at construction.

The mixed branch's inverse now takes the exponential under
np.errstate(over="ignore"), and nothing else changes: the lambda becomes
a nested function, which computes the same values. The branch with every
variable log-scaled does not meet it (its self-test takes an infinite
upper bound at 1e6, and a lower bound is positive), and keeps NumPy's
warning for a real overflow, beyond the largest float in the original
space.

Test: test_variable_transformer.py::
test_log_beside_linear_gives_no_overflow_warning: a log-scaled variable
on [1, 1000] beside a linear one with an infinite upper bound, and one on
[-1e3, 1e3], are built, and a point at 800 on the linear variable is
transformed and inverted, with warnings turned into errors; the
transformed bounds and the round trip are those of the transform. Both
cases fail at the parent on the RuntimeWarning (and pass there with the
warnings ignored) and pass here.
