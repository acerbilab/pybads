fix: integer-typed bounds and x0 are taken as floats, so that the log transform of a bound is not truncated (W2-45)

_bounds_check_ passed the bounds on with the dtype they were given.
VariableTransformer copies them and writes the log of the bounds of a
log-scaled variable into those copies in place, so an integer-typed
array was truncated: with lb = 1, ub = 1000, plb = 2, pub = 500 as
integer arrays at D = 2, the plausible box mapped to [-0.769, 1.072]
in u space instead of [-1, 1], and the hard bounds to [-1, 1.303]
instead of [-1.251, 1.251], and the GP, the mesh and the start on the
grid all followed. Since W2-3, a scalar bound is broadcast with its
dtype, so an integer scalar bound such as lower_bounds=1, refused at
D > 1 before, met the defect too. MATLAB BADS has no such case: its
numeric literals are doubles.

_bounds_check_ now converts x0 and the four bounds to float arrays,
with np.asarray(..., dtype=float), right after the test that they are
real valued, which a cast placed before it would defeat (a complex x0
is still refused). The checks before that point (shapes, a single
starting point, finite plausible bounds, real values) do not depend on
the dtype; every step after it, the other checks, non_box_cons's call
on the plausible bounds and VariableTransformer included, takes
floats. A float64 input is passed on as before, without a copy, so a
run with float bounds moves nothing.

Nothing in the setup writes into the arrays that the user passes, before
or after this change: __init__ copies x0, and the plausible bounds when
it defaults them to the hard ones; _bounds_check_ returns float bounds
as views of the user's arrays, which no step writes into, and
_init_optim_state_ replaces them by copies of VariableTransformer's,
which copies its inputs. Checked with 1-D and 2-D, float and integer
arrays, with and without plausible bounds: the user's arrays are equal
to their copies after BADS(...) and after optimize().

Test: test_bads_inputs.py::test_integer_bounds_are_taken_as_floats:
integer arrays (with the plausible bounds given and omitted, for a
log-scaled variable beside a linear one) and integer scalars at D = 2
(both variables log-scaled, both linear), with an integer x0, give the
same log flags, original and transformed bounds, x0 and start on the
grid as the same values as floats, all of them float arrays. All 4 fail
at the parent (the transformed bounds differ in the 3 cases with a
log-scaled variable, the returned arrays are integer in the linear one)
and pass here.
