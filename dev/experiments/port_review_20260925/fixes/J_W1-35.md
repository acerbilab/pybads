**What changed and why:**
- **`_re_evaluate_history_`:** after `local_gp_fitting`, a `needs_refit` marker on the GP it returns means the rebuild failed and that GP has no posterior.
  - A past iterate then gets `fval` and `fsd` NaN, as MATLAB's `reevaluateIterList` does (`bads.m:1378-1413`, `gpupdate.m:340-349`, `gppred.m:22-56`).
  - The current iterate, the last row, is not overwritten and keeps the estimate it was recorded with. This departs from MATLAB on purpose, as ruled: in MATLAB the incumbent becomes NaN here (`bads.m:1101-1104`).
  - The docstring gains one paragraph saying this.
- **The two choices:**
  - At the end of an iteration, the choice of a better iterate now uses `np.nanargmax` instead of `np.argmax`.
  - The final choice uses `np.nanargmin` instead of `np.argmin`.
  - Both now skip NaN, as MATLAB's `max` and `min` do (`bads.m:1108, 1144`).
  - Before, `argmax` picked any NaN, so one NaN blocked every real improvement. `argmin` on the history's object array kept a NaN at row 1 and so returned it.
- **Stall tests** (about lines 1452 and 2416): left as they were.

**How I checked that the last row is the incumbent's, at both call sites:**
- *By reading the code:*
  - Rows are recorded only at `poll_iteration`, under `do_poll_step or is_finished`, and `poll_iteration` only grows. So the history has `poll_iteration + 1` rows.
  - End of an iteration (line 1508): the call comes right after row `poll_iteration` is recorded from `self.u`, `yval`, `fval` and `fsd`, with nothing in between. Afterwards `fval` is read back from that row.
  - End of the run (line 1568): the loop ends only through `is_finished`. There is no `break`, and the restart branch is `pass`. The pass that sets `is_finished` records that row.
  - The only later change to the incumbent is the switch to a better iterate, and it happens only after a re-estimate at line 1508 in that same pass. No evaluation follows it, so the call at line 1568 then returns at once (`last_re_eval == func_count`).
- *Empirically* (scratch script `check_last_row.py`):
  - 40 noisy runs: levels 1 and 2, seeds 0-9, 150 evaluations, with and without every third rebuild of the re-estimate failing.
  - At all 337 re-estimates that did work (315 at line 1508, 22 at line 1568), the last row held the incumbent's `u`, `yval`, `fval` and `fsd`, and the row count minus one equalled `optim_state["iter"]`.
  - No mismatches, no crashes, and every result was finite.

**All-NaN slices:** these cannot occur under the ruling, so I added no fallback.
- Both slices contain the last row, since `poll_iteration >= 1` at both call sites.
- The last row's estimate is either a successful re-estimate or the incumbent's own estimate. gpyreg's `predict` clamps the variance at 0, so a successful re-estimate is finite.
- The incumbent's estimate is finite: it starts at a finite value (the observation, with `fsd` from `noise_size` or `S`) and moves only to estimates accepted as improvements, and a NaN never is one.
- So the last row's improvement at the end of an iteration is 0 (at `improvement_quantile` 0.5), and its `q_beta` is finite.
