# The PyBADS film

A narrated explainer of PyBADS 1.5 for scientists who fit models. It shows
what Bayesian optimization and direct search each do well and badly, how
BADS combines them, one real PyBADS run on a two-parameter landscape, and
how BADS has performed and been used. `film.html` draws it in the browser
from recorded runs. On the draft voice (Kokoro, `am_michael`) it runs
2 min 48 s. The animatic is complete with its score; the final voice comes
after feedback on the animatic.

- [STORYBOARD.md](STORYBOARD.md): the film line by line, what each shot
  shows, and the sources of the narration's claims.
- [NOTES.md](NOTES.md): the decisions behind the film, the landscape, the
  run, the illustrations and the record, and what was tried and set aside.

## How the page works

`film.html` holds a list of shots, `SHOTS`, one per line of narration, in
order. Each has the line's id and text, a camera, a `note` that says what it
shows, and a `dur`, the length in seconds over which its motion is designed.
`film.html?shot=K` draws shot K, counted from 1 (shots 1 to 5 are lines 1.1
to 1.5, and shot 31 is the end card), at the end of its motion, and `&t=T`
draws it T seconds into it. `film.html?film=1&T=T` draws the film at T
seconds of its timeline. Once a frame is drawn, the page's title starts with
`ready`.

On the timeline (`film_timeline.js`) a shot is on screen from the start of
its line to the start of the next, and its motion is stretched over that
span. At every line the shot before fades out while the new one fades in,
over 0.7 s (`XF`), both drawn into one frame: `KG` is the brightness of
everything drawn. A caption shows while its line is spoken. A shot may move
its camera (`cam` as a function of time) and its HUD. The run's shots replay
the trace step by step (`runSearches`, `runTry`), scene 3 replays its direct
search round by round (`pollRound`), scene 4 draws two panels in viewports
of their own, and scene 7 draws three tiles, then six charts, then a field
of points.

The page exposes `window.badsFilm`: `render(k, t)` draws the shot of index
k, from 0, at time t; `frame(T)` draws the film at T; `events()` returns
the film's events on its timeline; `shots` and `seconds` describe it. The
events come from the table `EVENTS`, which lists for each shot what it shows
and when, in the shot's own seconds: an evaluation landing (`eval`, with its
value), a search's try (`hit`, `miss`; `deep` for evaluation 20, `big` for
the try that lands near the bottom of the trench), a step of a poll (`tap`,
`taphit`), the mesh growing or shrinking (`grow`, `shrink`), a step of a
fast run (`tick`), and the film's moments (`rise`, `named`, `fooled`,
`poll`, `whoosh`, `search`, `lesson`, `reveal`, `you`, `pop`). `events()`
places them as the motion is placed. A change to a shot's motion needs the
same change in its `EVENTS` entry, or the score drifts from the picture.

## The data

The three data files are JavaScript assignments of JSON, generated and
committed, so that the page needs no server and computes no surrogate.

- `trace.js` is the run that scenes 4 to 6 replay: PyBADS with default
  options and `random_seed=25` on the film's landscape. The recorder
  subclasses `BADS` and wraps its search and poll steps, the target, and
  the acquisition function and ES classes; it only reads. The trace holds
  the evaluations, every SEARCH and POLL step with its tries and their
  predictions, and the surrogate at each state that a step used, renumbered
  in order. It was recorded with one BLAS thread, PyBADS at `149a24d1` and
  gpyreg 1.4.0 (`682585f7`), as its `meta` says; there, `pybads` is the
  editable install's stale version string and `pybads_revision` the one to
  trust. A run repeats bit for bit only on one machine with one BLAS
  thread, so the trace is kept rather than regenerated.
- `illus.js` holds the illustrations of scenes 2 and 3, computed on the same
  landscape from the run's initial design: a plain Bayesian optimization,
  with its surrogate after each step, and a plain coordinate direct search.
  It also holds the true minimum.
- `bench.js` holds the six charts of scene 7 (NOTES.md, "The record").

The surrogates are kept on 128 × 128 grids over the box [−5, 5]², quantized:
the mean on 16 bits between the grid's `z_lo` and `z_hi`, the standard
deviation on 8 bits as the square root of its fraction of `sd_hi`. Each grid
is stored as differences from its left neighbour along each row, its first
column as differences from the value above, and a 16-bit grid as its low
bytes followed by its high bytes. `decodeStates` in `film.html` undoes this.
Nothing is lost beyond the quantization, and git compresses the differences
to less than half the size of the plain values. The search's candidates of
each generation are not kept, as the film does not draw them.

`film.html` refers to the trace's steps, states and evaluations, and to the
illustrations' steps, by index: for instance `RUN.steps[17]`, the POLL of
line 5.4; the states `RS[12]`, `RS[13]`, `RS[17]` and `RS[18]`; `EV[19]`,
evaluation 20; `BS[0]` to `BS[16]`; `MPOLL[1]` and `MPOLL[4]` to
`MPOLL[6]`. The time-lapse of line 5.7 steps through every state. A new
trace or new illustrations need every index checked.

## Making the film

The media are not tracked. They go to the gitignored `dev/media/film/` of
the checkout that makes them, one folder per voice, `V` below; the draft
voice's is `dev/media/film/voice-am_michael/`. `V` holds the clips of the
lines (`voice/`), `narration.wav` and `narration.srt`, `whisper.txt`,
`events.json`, the score (`score.wav`, `mix.wav`, `stems/`,
`score_plan.txt`) and the videos (`film_silent.mp4`, `film.mp4`). A
worktree's ignored files go with it when it is removed, so move that folder
first. No take of a line can be made again: Kokoro does not give the same
take twice.

From `dev/film/`:

```console
python -u scripts/voice.py V                                  # Kokoro: V/voice/, V/narration.wav, V/narration.srt, film_timeline.js
python -u scripts/verify_voice.py V                           # every line as written? (Whisper)
node scripts/record.mjs V/events.json --film                  # the times the score follows
python -u scripts/score.py V                                  # V/score.wav, V/mix.wav (2 to 3 min)
node scripts/record.mjs V/film_silent.mp4 --film --crf 23     # the film at 1280 x 720 (about 15 min)
python scripts/mux.py V/film_silent.mp4 V/mix.wav V/film.mp4  # the sound under it, at -16 LUFS
node scripts/record.mjs OUT --shot 13 --times 2,5             # stills of shot 13, 2 s and 5 s into its line
node scripts/check.mjs                                        # syntax check of film.html's script
python -u scripts/export_trace.py --out OUT/trace_new.js --check   # the run again, compared bit for bit
python -u scripts/illustrations.py                            # illus.js
python -u scripts/bench_export.py --data DIR                  # bench.js
```

`voice.py` voices a line when `V` has no clip of it, when `--only` names the
line or its scene, or with `--all`. The pace is the narration's `speed`,
1.12, which a scene's `speed` overrides: 1.2 in scene 1, 1.15 in scene 2.
Voicing a line again moves every line after it, and the page places its
events relative to the lines, so they move with them. After any change to
the timing or to a shot's motion, export the events and make the score
again. The header of `record.mjs` lists its options: the size (default
1280 × 720), the frame rate (25), the span of a shot, and a shot's last
frame held for 0.6 s.

What each script needs:

- `record.mjs` and `check.mjs`: Node 22 or later. `record.mjs` also needs
  Chrome (`CHROME`, or its default install location) and ffmpeg (`FFMPEG`,
  or `ffmpeg` on the `PATH`; `pip install --target DIR imageio-ffmpeg` puts
  one under `DIR/imageio_ffmpeg/binaries/` without touching an
  environment). It serves `dev/film/` itself; no server needs to run.
- `voice.py`: kokoro 0.9.4 or later, soundfile and SciPy, in an environment
  of its own (Python 3.12, CPU torch). Kokoro fetches its weights from the
  Hugging Face hub into `HF_HOME`.
- `verify_voice.py`: faster-whisper, whose model `small.en` comes the same
  way.
- `score.py` and `mux.py`: NumPy, SciPy and soundfile; `mux.py` also an
  ffmpeg with `loudnorm`, which that of `imageio-ffmpeg` has.
- `export_trace.py` and `illustrations.py`: the repository's development
  environment, with gpyreg selected as `AGENTS.md` says, and one BLAS thread
  (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1`).
  `export_trace.py` writes a new trace elsewhere, to compare before it
  replaces `trace.js`; `--check` reruns the seed without the recorder, in
  the same process, and compares the evaluations bit for bit.
- `bench_export.py`: NumPy and SciPy, and the 2017 benchmark's result files.

## The score

The score is tracker-style music in E minor near 96 BPM: a few voices, a
pulsing bass, a cold pad and a sparse lead. `synth.py`, the score of the
film's style frame, holds its instruments, levels and mix, and `score.py`
imports it as a module and sets the form. The full arrangement (drums,
bass, pad, arpeggio) plays while the film trusts a surrogate: scene 2, and
every SEARCH. A low drone with dry taps, one per step, plays while it steps
without one: scene 3, and every POLL. The flat second of the Phrygian mode
(F over E, then F major 7 ♯11) colours the surrogate being fooled, at lines
2.4 and 5.3. A build on B7sus4 leads to the search that lands near the
bottom of the trench (line 5.6), where the harmony lifts to C major 7 and
the lead enters; the lift (C major 7, G, D sus2) carries the record and
resolves to E minor when the version pops on the end card.

The form is a run of sections, each a fixed number of bars, that start at
lines of the film and at three events: the search near the bottom of the
trench, the reveal of the true landscape and the version's pop. The tempo
of each section fits its bars to its span, between 92 and 96 BPM on the
draft voice, with a ritardando into the pop, so that every scene and those
three moments fall on a downbeat. Every event sounds on the nearest
thirty-second note, at most 40 ms from the picture; a fast run keeps one
event per thirty-second note. An evaluation sounds as a glassy tick pitched
by the height of its ground, a search that succeeds as a bell (the lower it
lands, the higher the bell), one that fails as a thud with a dissonant stab,
a step of a poll as a dry tap, an octave lower while the mesh is large, and
the mesh growing or shrinking as a note that glides an octave down or up.

While the voice speaks the music dips by 45 % and the events and effects by
25 % (`DUCK`). `SCORE_LU` sets the score's loudness after the ducking, in LU
from the voice's: −17, at which the voice stands 12 to 28 dB above the
score, line by line. A first mix at −15 LU was slightly too loud, at times
distracting. `mux.py` brings the mix to −16 LUFS integrated and −1.5 dBTP
with two passes of ffmpeg's `loudnorm`, copies the video stream and cuts the
sound to the video's length.

## Checks

- `node scripts/check.mjs` after every edit of `film.html`, and stills of
  the shots changed.
- `verify_voice.py` after voicing: it lists the lines whose words differ
  from the script, ignoring case and punctuation and taking the voice's
  spellings and Whisper's ways of writing them ("PyBADS", "pie bads",
  "BADS", "bads") as the same, writes every transcript to `V/whisper.txt`,
  and exits with status 1 when any line differs. A short line out of its
  context can be misheard; listen to it before voicing it again.
- `score.py` prints the tempo of each section, the largest and the average
  distance of an event from its picture, and the loudness of the voice, of
  the score and of each stem; `V/score_plan.txt` lists every bar with its
  time, tempo, chord and patterns, and the events that sound in it. A much
  faster or slower take can push a section's tempo out of the 90s.
- `mux.py` prints the loudness and true peak it measured and those of the
  film it wrote.
